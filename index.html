Amogarian

The written language of Valrion, and the one lettering the DRI never wears in front of humans. Type below to see any text set in the script, on the true crest's ring and in a line.

How the script works

The script has 26 letters, one for each letter of the English alphabet, plus two single glyphs for th and sh. Every glyph follows a small set of rules, so a reader who learns the rules can guess unfamiliar ones.

Vowels are round. Every vowel is built from a circle or a curve. The stroke that leaves the circle (up, down, or through it) tells you which vowel it is. W, the half-vowel, is drawn from curves too: a double cup, like the letter's name.
Consonants are cut. Every consonant is a straight spine with angled branches, and none of them contain a circle. Only x drops the spine.
A dot above means voiced. Six consonants come in pairs, and the voiced one is the same shape with a dot on top: k/g, t/d, p/b, s/z, f/v, c/j. Here c is the ch sound and j is its voiced partner.
Two sounds get their own glyph. th and sh are single glyphs, not two. Typing ch in the transliterator gives the single glyph c.
Direction. On a line, the script runs left to right. On a ring, such as the crest, it runs clockwise with the tops of the glyphs pointing outward.
Numbers are base five, in honour of the five stars (see below).

Other consonants, and w

Doubled sounds, like the rr in Vorrbanisk, are written by repeating the glyph. The transliterator above works letter for letter, so any English name can be written. Note that the sound of c is ch, so "Carl" would be pronounced "Charl" if read aloud in Amogarian.

Numbers

Counting is in base five, and the digits are made of dots. Five is therefore written "10," the way we write ten. That makes five the "round number" of the language, which suits a species that named its mission after five stars.

Grammar in brief

  • Plural: add -ai. ixel (star) becomes ixelai (stars).
  • Adjectives follow the noun and never change: ixel nolum, "silent star."
  • Prepositions come before the noun: ruvath zarun, "over the world."
  • Verbs take a person prefix: ne- I, du- you, ka- we, so- they. So kavael is "we watch."
  • Tense: add -uth for the past and -ora for the future: kavaeluth (we watched), kavaelora (we will watch).
  • Negation: put ol before the verb: ol kavael, "we do not watch."
  • Numbers come before the noun: thavor ixelai, "five stars."
  • Articles: none. zarun means "world," "a world," or "the world," depending on context.

Notes for the lore

  • Pronunciation. x sounds like "ks," y is a short "i" as in "sit" (which is how Nyxtar comes out as NIK-star), and r is lightly rolled.
  • Suggested etymology. Valrion reads as val + rion, "the high seat." Mora (to return) could be where the DRI's MORA anchor gets its name.
  • A story option. The Vorrbanisk were banished from Valrion, so their writing could be a corrupted variant: the same letters, but with the voicing dot moved to the bottom of every consonant. Readers who know the rules would spot it on sight.
  • Still open. The names Nyxtar, Ralixnth, and Zeralyth are set here as spelled in your document, and no meanings are assigned, since the story may want to keep those unexplained.
